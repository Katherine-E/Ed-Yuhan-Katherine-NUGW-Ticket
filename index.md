  <h1>Our collective vision for the upcoming year</h1>
  <p>
    We believe that securing the best possible contract requires the engagement of every worker in our union. **You deserve transparency and the tools to shape your union’s policies and priorities—and this is the best path to winning the contract you need and deserve.** If you have any questions, comments, or concerns, reach out to any of us!
  </p>

  <h2>Building a member-run union</h2>
  
  <h3>Member-led organizing and decision-making</h3>
  <p>Our members are the highest authority in our union. This means using town halls and union meetings as a space for debate and sharing perspectives. Disagreements are natural for a group of three thousand workers. We must foster an open and respectful environment for discussions. We will make our General Membership Meetings (GMMs) a member-first space by facilitating discussions and actively collaborating with members to ensure you have the space and agency to make the best decisions for NUGW. As leadership, we will not make decisions behind closed doors. Instead, we will bring proposals to members for drafting and discussion before voting and encourage workers to create meeting agendas and focus our meetings on rank-and-file members’ issues and concerns. And we take your feedback seriously: in the last few months alone, we led efforts to change course on out-of-unit member voting rights and dues enforcement policies after feedback from members and stewards. <b>Bargaining and union operations should be clear, transparent, and the product of conversations with workers.</b></p>

  <h3>Building coalitions around our common struggle</h3>
  <p>Our NUGW working groups are at the center of our fight. These groups—international workers’ rights (DIAS), accessibility and healthcare, trans rights, and Title IX—lead the organizing and the fight for our structurally marginalized workers’ demands at the bargaining table. We are closely integrated into these groups and support their efforts. When DIAS pushed NU to reimburse passport fees, we organized actions with DIAS to turn this “unwinnable” fight into an inspiring victory. When the Accessibility & Healthcare Working Group reignited organizing on dependent healthcare, we supported their efforts to elevate dependent healthcare into our union-wide consciousness. We are proud that <b>chairs from every working group have endorsed our ticket</b>b – and we plan to take their lead in our fight at the bargaining table. Our campaign also represents a university-wide coalition: members and organizers from every division have endorsed us, and we are successfully expanding our collective union power to under-organized departments. <b>We are working with you to build the cross-campus solidarity that we need to win at the bargaining table.</b> </p>

  <h2>Preparing for our contract fight</h2>
  <h3> Hope is Power </h3>
  <p>We build hope by talking to one another and fighting for each other. We can envision a transformative contract—and then fighting together to win it. As graduate workers, we deserve to thrive. We are faced with a federal administration that is openly hostile to academia, our international workers, and our trans colleagues, as well as a university administration that prefers to line its pockets rather than to protect its workers. Our strength and hope to resist lies in our collective power. Among other benefits, our contract's job security and trans protections shielded us from the worst of NU’s deal with the federal government. Now, that contract expires in six months. So, it is all hands on deck to defend what we have and fight for a just workplace for current and future generations of grad workers. Our guiding light in contract negotiations will be your will and priorities. From the bargaining survey and our walkthroughs, we know we must fight to keep our current contract benefits, including dental and vision insurance, the International Employee Support Fund, and the right to an inclusive work environment. We know that we must fight for a better second contract which includes a fair pay increase, job and funding security (e.g., prevent unfair dismissals and restoring banked quarters), expanded protections for international workers (e.g. ensuring a fair path to degree completion in case of visa denials or deportations), subsidized transportation costs, and full coverage of dependent healthcare premiums. Winning these protections would transform our lives at Northwestern. As experienced leaders and organizers, we know how to empower workers to fight through democratic organizing. <b>Our contract fight begins with hope and trust in one another, and when we each choose to stand up together, we can win.</b></p>

  <h3>Centering our workers in strike preparedness</h3>
  <p>We will only win a strong contract if we have the power to back it up. We as workers have no greater power than in withholding our labor. Striking will and should look different based on what work looks like for specific programs. Instead of imposing a common definition of what a strike should be, we envision working closely with members and stewards to determine what it means in your program to simultaneously maximize pressure on the university administration while reducing risk and fear in our coworkers. This means we will host town halls and train stewards across departments to have open and honest conversations about striking with every member and develop the tools and resources to build trust and strike power. We also plan to form a standing committee dedicated to leading the research and training efforts to ensure that a credible strike threat is attainable. <b>We only win what we are organized to fight for.</b>  </p>

  <h3>Protecting marginalized workers</h3>
  <p>We as NUGW are an antiracist, feminist labor union and these principles continue to guide us as we continually stay aware of what each individual's boundaries may be, given our political climate. All actions we take as a union must carefully consider the risks to our most vulnerable coworkers, including (but not limited to) international and trans workers. Just as we invite members to rally their departments and organize strikes, we will provide workers with our assessment of the risks of each action as well as the tools and strategies to protect themselves. We must also ensure that every member has access to union representation, resources, and a voice at the bargaining table, regardless of how visible they are able or willing to be. <b>This begins by strengthening and empowering our steward and organizer network to speak up and take action with their departments and labs.</b></p>
