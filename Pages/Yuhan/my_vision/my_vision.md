# Vice President for Membership's Vision

## Connect and coordinate across campus
The VP is for membership. The most important thing is to make sure that people are willing or even excited to join organizing, or at least take the responsibility of paying dues. Along the spectrum of being enthusiastic and doing the bare minimum of paying dues, there’re so many different levels of involvement. My job is to work with the stewards and organizers at every level (campus chief, division chief, local stewards, department organizers) to inspire everyone to take one step beyond where they’re currently at.

I want to build communities and make union work fun. It will always be a serious job at the end of the day, but we need to do our best to create a fun and friendly atmosphere to draw people in and keep them engaged.

## Tighten the team before bargaining
We need to foster communication within our union. Asking everyone to attend a million meetings is impossible. It’s my job to stay on top of different working groups, attend meetings and check in with group leaders. I will work with them to figure out what the priorities are for each group and figure out how we can help each other in achieving the big picture of a strong contract and organized union.

I will also place trust in our members—they know what they are doing. As leaders our job is to support working groups and departments to organize for what they need, as they know their own strengths and struggles better than anyone else.

## Organize information, make sure they’re in clear structure and accessible to everyone

After getting people excited about our collective work, it’s our job to make things easier for everyone.Above all, our folders and calendars need to be reorganized! There are so many folders with different access flying around. 

- Meeting agendas, notes, decisions, and action items should be consistently documented and updated; and
- Information should be incorporated into a larger shared overview, so members can quickly and easily understand what different working groups and organizers are doing.

I will really encourage people to stay tuned but I also don’t expect everyone to read every document. It’s our job to throw targeted information at different audiences while organizing information to ensure proactive members can quickly and easily find information for themselves.

## Listen to our members and meet them where they are
We need to meet everyone where they are comfortable, and understand their risks and safety levels. We need to take concrete and consistent actions to protect members (e.g., strict consent policy on media, clear data privacy rules) to make sure members feel empowered and safe as they mobilize and organize. We need to do research to understand legal and practical risks and protections, and share them with members. We need to make sure we know where members’ boundaries are, meet them where they are, then encourage them to step up together. By listening and working together, we can turn fear into courage. 

## Prepare for bargaining and a potential strike
We need to do research to know what people really need and want. Actively listen and let members talk. Bring surveys. Go on walkthroughs. Leaders should never miss the frontline work. When we let people voice their opinions, they’ll pay more attention to the work and get more and more invested. When we share information with people at walkthroughs and discussions, we use it as an opportunity to motivate them and encourage them to participate. At the end of the day, you are the best person to fight for your own rights. We need to convey the message that the contract fight is not just the work of NUGW leadership. It is OUR contract therefore OUR responsibility and opportunity to contribute.

At the same time, we have to really listen to people about what’s stopping them from participating or going on a strike. We must not take people’s sacrifice for granted. Understand the nature of each department’s work. TAing? Experiments? Remote work? Keeping animals alive or maintaining equipment? Motivate people and figure out what’s at stake for them. Identify the fine line between contributing as much as they can and keeping them safe and in good academic standing.
