# Campus Chief Steward's Vision

As Campus Chief Steward (CCS), I will work closely with our Division Chief Stewards (DCS) to build an organizing infrastructure to fight for the contract we deserve. This involves at least three big focuses: 

## We must recruit stewards and organizers in every department ahead of our bargaining fight
This year, it is more important than ever to have active organizers in every department. I will work closely with the DCSs and existing stewards (who know their divisions more intimately than I do) to ensure we have 
organizers in every department and we are in dialogue with as many workers as possible. To do so, we will leverage our existing connections with members, and conduct walkthroughs and cold outreach in otherwise disengaged 
programs with as many members as possible. This also means having conversations with these members to understand their capacities and provide organizing and steward training.  


## We must be organized for and during our contract fight
We use our connections and stewards to hear what our members care about. I will work with the bargaining committee to facilitate communicating and gathering input from our members. This will look like walkthroughs which 
target a specific article of the contract, utilizing research from our Contract Action Team (CAT) research groups and working groups, and town halls to discuss ongoing bargaining. Additionally if time and capacity permits 
I envision doing a more detailed bargaining survey that goes beyond asking workers their priorities and asks them their needs for this contract fight. Our bargaining will need to be as transparent as possible to membership 
because that is what they deserve and it is how we maximize member engagement. I see CCS as the information conduit for this transparency. I will work with the DCSs to make sure as stewards we are speaking directly to 
workers, that no concern is being overlooked and no one feels silenced or underrepresented. I must also make sure this information is getting to the BC through myself and the DCSs. Having membership input will never be 
useful unless we are listening to it, and having bargaining information available isn't meaningful unless we ensure it's accessible to our workers. 

## We must continue our grievance campaigns whether it be for unit exclusions, discipline and discharge, or any other issue our workers are facing
Not only are grievances an opportunity to protect and empower workers but they are critical evidence at the bargaining table and prove to the administration that we will not give up on fighting for our workers just 
because bargaining is around the corner. Then after we have our new contract we must make it as easy to understand for members as possible, this includes holding town halls, training, communications, info graphics and 
flyers. Our goal is to empower workers to know their rights under the contract so that they can know when and how to file a grievance if that contract is violated. I envision this will involve much collaboration between 
myself, the grievance committee, and stewards so we can continue to use our organizing skills to protect workers under their contract.
